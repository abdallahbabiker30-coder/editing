Question 115 points
CURVILINEAR MOTION – NORMAL & TANGENTIAL COMPONENTS
Given: v = 400 m/s, a = 50 m/s², with a at 70° from the direction of motion (the tangent t), pointing toward the center of curvature.
Find: the rate of increase in speed, v̇ = at (5 p), and the radius of curvature ρ (10 p).

Rate of increase in speed (tangential component) – 5 p

The rate of change of speed is the tangential component of the acceleration:

v̇ = at = a·cos 70° = 50·cos 70° = 50(0.3420)
v̇ = at = 17.1 m/s²

Radius of curvature (normal component) – 10 p

an = a·sin 70° = 50(0.9397) = 46.98 m/s²

Then use an = v²/ρ:

ρ = v² / an = (400)² / 46.98 = 160 000 / 46.98
ρ = 3405 m ≈ 3.41 km

Check: √(at² + an²) = √(17.10² + 46.98²) = 50.0 m/s² ✓

Question 220 points
EQUATION OF MOTION – NORMAL & TANGENTIAL COORDINATES
Given: m = 1 kg, constant speed v = 4 m/s, μs = 0.6, g = 9.81 m/s²
Find: the smallest radius of curvature ρmin so that the cartons do not slip.

Equations of motion

ΣFb = 0:   N − mg = 0  ⇒  N = mg = (1)(9.81) = 9.81 N
ΣFt = m·at = 0   (constant speed)
ΣFn = m·an:   Ff = m·v²/ρ

Condition for no slipping

The smallest radius is reached when friction is at its maximum (the carton is about to slip):

Ff = μsN = (0.6)(9.81) = 5.886 N
μs·m·g = m·v²/ρmin  ⇒  ρmin = v² / (μsg) = (4)² / [(0.6)(9.81)] = 16 / 5.886
ρmin = 2.72 m

The mass cancels out, so the answer does not depend on the carton's mass. If ρ < 2.72 m, the friction needed would be more than μsN and the carton would slip outward.

THEORY

a) Difference between kinematics and kinetics (7 p)

KinematicsKinetics
DefinitionStudies the geometry of motion: how a body moves, without considering the forces that cause the motion.Studies the relation between the forces acting on a body and the motion they produce.
QuantitiesPosition, displacement, velocity, acceleration and time.Force, mass, moment, momentum, work and energy, together with acceleration.
Main equationsv = ds/dt, a = dv/dt, a ds = v dv, at = v̇, an = v²/ρNewton's 2nd law ΣF = m·a, work–energy, impulse–momentum
ExampleQuestion 1: finding at and ρ from v and a.Question 2: using friction (a force) to find ρmin.

In short: kinematics answers “how does it move?” and kinetics answers “why does it move that way?” Kinetics uses the results of kinematics.

b) Conservation of linear momentum (7 p)

The linear momentum of a particle is L = m·v (a vector, units kg·m/s). The principle of linear impulse and momentum says:

m·v₁ + Σ∫ F dt = m·v₂

If the sum of the external impulses on a system of particles is zero, then the total linear momentum of the system stays constant:

Σ m·v₁ = Σ m·v₂     (e.g. mAvA1 + mBvB1 = mAvA2 + mBvB2)
  • Condition: there are no external impulsive forces on the system, or they are negligible. The internal forces between the particles always cancel in pairs (Newton's 3rd law: equal and opposite), so they do not change the total momentum.
  • Momentum is a vector, so it can be conserved in one direction (e.g. x) even if it is not conserved in another.
  • Applications: collisions and impact, explosions, gun and bullet recoil, a person jumping off a boat, and coupling of railway cars.
  • Note: momentum is conserved in a collision, but kinetic energy usually is not, because some of it is lost as heat, sound and deformation.
