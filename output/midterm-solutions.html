INTRODUCTION TO THE DESIGN PROCESS

(a) Sequential stages of the mechanical design process (6 pts)

  1. Identification of a need: design always starts from a need, e.g. a problem to solve or a product to improve. It is turned into a clear problem statement with the required functions, loads, constraints, cost and so on.
  2. Design: the engineer generates concepts, chooses materials and geometry, and does the analysis (stress, deflection, failure theories, factor of safety). The design is then evaluated and optimized, and the result is drawings and specifications.
  3. Manufacture: the part is produced from the design, using the chosen processes, tolerances and assembly.
  4. Performance assessment: the manufactured part is tested and checked in service to see whether it meets the original need (strength, life, function, safety).

(b) Why the design process is iterative (6 pts)

The process is iterative because the first design is rarely the best one. Information that is only available later, such as manufacturing difficulties, test results or service behaviour, often shows weaknesses that could not be fully predicted at the start. Each stage feeds back into the earlier ones, and the cycle repeats until the product meets the need.

If a problem is identified:

  • During manufacturing: go back to the design stage and modify the design (geometry, tolerances, material, process) so that it can be manufactured.
  • During performance assessment: find the cause, then go back to manufacture (if the fault is in production quality) or to design (if the design itself is inadequate). Re-analyse, correct it and assess again.

(c) Five mechanical failure modes (8 pts)

Failure modePhysical meaning
1. YieldingThe stress goes above the yield strength Sy, so the part deforms permanently (plastically) and no longer keeps its shape or function. This is the main failure mode for ductile materials under static load.
2. FractureThe part breaks into pieces when the stress reaches the ultimate strength. It is typical of brittle materials, which fail suddenly with little deformation.
3. BucklingA slender member under compression suddenly bends sideways (loses stability) at a critical load, even though the stress may still be below Sy.
4. CreepSlow, time-dependent permanent deformation under a constant load, mainly at high temperature. It can end in rupture.
5. FatigueFailure under repeated or fluctuating (dynamic) loads. A crack starts and grows over many cycles at stresses below the static strength.
(extra) Chemical failure – corrosion / wearMaterial is lost from the surface by chemical attack (corrosion) or by rubbing contact (wear). This reduces the section and the strength over time.

RA = RB = 40 kN (upward, the loads are symmetric)

Bending moment:

0 ≤ x ≤ 0.2:   M = 40x  →  M(0.2) = 8 kN·m
0.2 ≤ x ≤ 0.8:   M = 40x − 40(x − 0.2) = 8 kN·m (constant; the shear force V = 0)

Mmax = RA·a = (40 kN)(0.2 m)
Mmax = 8 kN·m (constant between the two loads)

Tinternal = 40 kN·m (for 0.2 m ≤ x ≤ 0.8 m)
