Chapter 1 · Introduction and Stress Analysis

1. The design process

Design always starts from a need. After the part is designed and made, its performance is checked. If a problem appears, you go back to manufacturing or to the design.

Failure of mechanical parts can take many forms: yielding/fracture, buckling, chemical failure (corrosion), creep and fatigue.

Types of loading: Static loading means the load is constant with time. Dynamic loading means the load varies with time, which leads to fatigue.

2. Stress, strain and Hooke's law

  • Stress = force per unit area. There are two types: normal stress σ (perpendicular to the surface) and shear stress τ (parallel to the surface).
  • Normal strain ε = change in length / original length. Shear strain γ = change in angle.
  • Elastic region (Hooke's law):
σ = E·ε      τ = G·γ      (E = Young's modulus, G = shear modulus)

3. Stresses in a solid round bar (diameter d, outer radius c = d/2)

4. Principal stresses and 3-D Mohr circles

The stress state at a point is the stress tensor. The principal stresses σ₁, σ₂, σ₃ are its eigenvalues, so you find them by solving:

| σx−σ   τxy   τxz ;   τxy   σy−σ   τyz ;   τxz   τyz   σz−σ | = 0  ⇒  σ₁, σ₂, σ₃
Three-dimensional Mohr circles (σ₁ ≥ σ₂ ≥ σ₃)
τmax = max( |σ₁−σ₂|/2 , |σ₂−σ₃|/2 , |σ₁−σ₃|/2 ) = (σ₁ − σ₃)/2

Example 1 – Loaded cantilever cylinder

A solid cylinder, d = 50 mm and L = 250 mm, is fixed at one end. Its free end carries an axial force F = 1 kN, a transverse force V = 3 kN and a torque T = 1 kN·m. Find the stresses at point A (top, at the wall) and point B (side, on the neutral axis, at the wall).

Section properties: A = 1.96×10⁻³ m², I = 3.07×10⁻⁷ m⁴, J = 6.14×10⁻⁷ m⁴, c = 0.025 m

  • Axial force: σA = σB = −F/A = −1000 / 1.96×10⁻³ = −0.509 MPa. It is the same at every point. The minus sign is used because the notes take F as compressive.
  • Shear force: τA = 0 (top). At B: τ = 4V/3A = 4(3000) / 3(1.96×10⁻³) = 2.04 MPa.
  • Torsion: τA = τB = Tc/J = 1000(0.025) / 6.14×10⁻⁷ = 40.7 MPa.
  • Bending at the wall: M = V·L = 3 kN × 0.25 m = 0.75 kN·m. σA = Mc/I = 750(0.025) / 3.07×10⁻⁷ = 61.1 MPa, and σB = 0 (neutral axis).

Combined stresses:

At A:   σx = σF + σM = −0.509 + 61.1 = 60.6 MPa,   τ = τT = 40.7 MPa
At B:   σx = −0.509 MPa,   τ = τV + τT = 2.04 + 40.7 = 42.8 MPa
Check with your professor: the notes write the torsion stress as 0.04 MPa (and so 2.08 MPa at B). That is the result if T = 1 N·m is used instead of 1000 N·m. With T = 1 kN·m, the correct torsion stress is 40.7 MPa. The steps are the same either way.

Example 2 – Principal stresses of a 3-D stress element

Given: σx = 20 MPa, σy = −10 MPa, σz = 15 MPa, τyz = 5 MPa (all other shear stresses are 0).

| 20−σ   0   0 ;   0   −10−σ   5 ;   0   5   15−σ | = 0
(20−σ)·[(−10−σ)(15−σ) − 25] = 0
(20−σ)·(σ² − 5σ − 175) = 0

The first factor gives σ = 20. The quadratic gives σ = (5 ± √725)/2 = 15.96 or −10.96.

σ₁ = 20 MPa,   σ₂ = 15.96 MPa,   σ₃ = −10.96 MPa
τmax = (σ₁ − σ₃)/2 = (20 + 10.96)/2 = 15.48 MPa

Chapter 2 · Static Failure Theories

7. The three failure theories (yield strength Sy, factor of safety n)

TheorySafe conditionFactor of safety
Case 1 – Maximum normal stressmax(|σ₁|, |σ₂|, |σ₃|) < Syn = Sy / max|σi|
Case 2 – Maximum shear stress (Tresca)max(|σ₁−σ₂|, |σ₂−σ₃|, |σ₃−σ₁|) < Syn = Sy / (σ₁ − σ₃)
Case 3 – Distortion energy (von Mises)σ' = √{ ½[(σ₁−σ₂)² + (σ₂−σ₃)² + (σ₃−σ₁)²] } < Syn = Sy / σ'

Von Mises written with stress components:

σ' = (1/√2)·√[ (σx−σy)² + (σy−σz)² + (σz−σx)² + 6(τxy² + τyz² + τzx²) ]
Plane stress (σ₃ = 0):   σ' = √(σ₁² − σ₁σ₂ + σ₂²) < Sy    → this is an ellipse
Bar with σ and τ only:   σ' = √(σ² + 3τ²)

Tresca vs von Mises

  • Pure shear (only τxy): von Mises gives σ' = √3·τxy = Sy, so the shear yield strength is Ssy = Sy/√3 = 0.577 Sy.
  • Tresca assumes Ssy = 0.5 Sy, which is about 15 % lower than von Mises.
  • So Tresca is more conservative (safer). Its safe region is a hexagon that sits inside the von Mises ellipse.
Plane-stress safe regions: Tresca hexagon (dark) inside the von Mises ellipse (light)

8. Summary of the failure procedure

  1. Choose the most critical point of the part and work out all its stresses: σ = (σx, σy, σz, τxy, τyz, τzx).
  2. Find the principal stresses σ₁, σ₂, σ₃ as the eigenvalues of the stress tensor (the determinant above).
  3. Pick a failure theory (Case 1, 2 or 3) and require its equivalent stress to be smaller than Sy.
  4. Factor of safety: n = Sy / (equivalent stress).

Example 3 – Factor of safety at a point

Given: σx = 40 MPa, σy = 30 MPa, τxy = 20 MPa, τyz = 10 MPa, Sy = 100 MPa (σz = τxz = 0). Find n using Tresca and von Mises.

Step 1 – Principal stresses

| 40−σ   20   0 ;   20   30−σ   10 ;   0   10   −σ | = 0
(40−σ)[(30−σ)(−σ) − 100] − 20[20(−σ)] = 0
σ³ − 70σ² + 700σ + 4000 = 0
σ₁ = 56.31 MPa,   σ₂ = 17.71 MPa,   σ₃ = −4.01 MPa   (check: the sum is 70 = σx+σy+σz ✓)

Step 2 – Differences: |σ₁−σ₂| = 38.60, |σ₂−σ₃| = 21.72, |σ₁−σ₃| = 60.32 MPa

Step 3 – Tresca

max difference = 60.32 MPa  ⇒  n = 100 / 60.32 = 1.66

Step 4 – von Mises

σ' = √{ ½[38.60² + 21.72² + 60.32²] } = √(½ × 5600) = 52.9 MPa  ⇒  n = 100 / 52.9 = 1.89

Tresca gives the smaller n, so it is the more conservative of the two.

Example 4 – Thick-walled cylinder (closed ends, internal pressure Pi)

The critical point is the inner radius, where the stresses are:

σr = −Pi    σθ = Pi(Ro² + Ri²)/(Ro² − Ri²)    σz = PiRi²/(Ro² − Ri²)

Here Ro is unknown. To see the method, take the thickness also equal to 100 mm, so Ri = 100 mm and Ro = 200 mm:

σr = −Pi,   σθ = (40000+10000)/(40000−10000)·Pi = (5/3)Pi,   σz = 10000/30000·Pi = (1/3)Pi

The largest difference is between σθ and σr, so Tresca gives:

n = Sy / |σθ − σr| = 2   with   σθ − σr = 2PiRo²/(Ro² − Ri²)

Method: put in Pi, Sy and n, solve this equation for the unknown Ro, then the wall thickness is t = Ro − Ri.

Example 5 – Shaft diameter using von Mises (Sy = 400 MPa)

The shaft has bearings at both ends with spans of 200 mm – 600 mm – 200 mm. It carries gear/pulley forces and transmits a torque T. Find the diameter d using von Mises and a factor of safety n.

At the critical section there is a shear force F, a torque T and a bending moment M.

  • T causes shear stress at all points on the surface: τ = Tc/J = 16T/πd³.
  • M causes the largest normal stress at A and C (top and bottom): σ = Mc/I = 32M/πd³.
  • The shear force F causes shear stress at B and D (4F/3A). It is small, so it is neglected.
  • So the critical points are A and C.
σ' = √(σ² + 3τ²) = (16/πd³)·√(4M² + 3T²) = Sy/n

d = [ 16·n·√(4M² + 3T²) / (π·Sy) ]1/3

Steps: (1) draw the bending moment diagram and read Mmax; (2) get T from the power or gear forces; (3) put M, T, Sy = 400 MPa and n into the formula; (4) round d up to a standard size.

The actual force and torque values in the notes were not readable in the photo. Use the numbers from the sheet with the formula above.
