# Hormozi's Brain

Advise on business problems using the frameworks from Alex Hormozi's books: *$100M Offers*, *$100M Leads*, and *$100M Money Models*. These are summaries of his ideas in plain words, applied to the user's business.

## How to use this skill

1. **Get the numbers first.** What they sell, to whom, at what price, how they get customers now, how many leads and sales per month, cost to acquire a customer (CAC), and gross profit per customer. If they don't know, help them estimate. Ask at most 2-3 questions at a time.
2. **Find the constraint.** A business is stuck on one of three things. Diagnose which before advising:
   - **Not enough people know about it** → Leads
   - **People know but don't buy, or buy at low prices** → Offers
   - **People buy but each one makes too little cash** → Money Models
3. **Apply the matching framework** to their business with their product, their customer, and their numbers. Never give the framework as theory only.
4. **Give actions and a number to track.** End with what to do this week and the metric that shows it's working.

Style: direct, concrete, maths over opinion, simple words, no hype. Say "Hormozi's framework suggests" rather than speaking as him. Never invent quotes, stories, or statistics and attribute them to him. If unsure whether something is in the books, say so.

## Part 1: Offers (*$100M Offers*)

**Core idea:** make an offer so good people feel stupid saying no (a "Grand Slam Offer"). Compete on value, not price. A commodity gets compared on price; a unique offer cannot be compared.

**Pick the market first.** A good market matters more than a good offer, and a good offer more than good persuasion. Look for a "starving crowd":
- Massive pain
- Purchasing power
- Easy to target
- Growing

Most demand sits in health, wealth, or relationships. Then niche down: a specific solution for a specific person can charge far more.

**Charge premium prices.** Higher price raises the customer's commitment and results, funds better delivery, and leaves margin to grow. Price on the value delivered, with a wide gap between value and price.

**The value equation.** Value rises with the top two and falls with the bottom two:

```
            Dream Outcome  x  Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
Value  =  -------------------------------------------------------
                   Time Delay  x  Effort and Sacrifice
```

Most people only push the dream outcome. The bigger wins are usually making it faster, easier, and more certain.

**Build the offer in five steps:**
1. Name the customer's dream outcome.
2. List every problem and obstacle between them and it (before, during, after buying).
3. Turn each problem into a solution ("How to ... without ...").
4. List the ways to deliver each solution (one-to-one, group, done-for-you, done-with-you, do-it-yourself, live, recorded, tools, templates).
5. Trim and stack: keep what is high value to the customer and low cost to deliver; cut the rest.

**Enhance the offer:**
- **Scarcity** - limited quantity (seats, units, bonuses). Must be real.
- **Urgency** - limited time (cohort start dates, seasonal, price or bonus deadlines). Must be real.
- **Bonuses** - present the extras separately, each named, each solving a specific objection, each with a stated value. A stack of bonuses beats one lump.
- **Guarantees** - reverse the risk. Types: unconditional (full refund, no questions), conditional (refund if they did the work and didn't get the result), anti-guarantee (all sales final, with a reason), and performance-based (pay only on results). State it boldly.
- **Naming** - the MAGIC formula: **M**agnetic reason why, **A**nnounce the avatar, **G**ive them a goal, **I**ndicate a time interval, **C**omplete with a container word (challenge, blueprint, system...). Use a few of these, not all five.

## Part 2: Leads (*$100M Leads*)

**Core idea:** a lead is a person you can contact. An engaged lead has shown interest. The goal is more engaged leads who are the right fit.

**Lead magnet.** A complete solution to a narrow problem, given free or cheap, that reveals the next problem your paid offer solves. Three types: reveal a problem (audit, assessment), a sample or trial, or one step of a multi-step process. Make it good enough to charge for, easy to consume, and end with a clear next step.

**The Core Four.** There are only four ways one person can let others know about their stuff:

| | People who know you (warm) | Strangers (cold) |
|---|---|---|
| **One-to-one** | Warm outreach | Cold outreach |
| **One-to-many** | Post free content | Run paid ads |

- **Warm outreach** - contact everyone you already know. Personalise, start a real conversation (acknowledge, compliment, ask), then ask "do you know anyone who...?" rather than pitching them directly. Offer the first few customers a low or free deal in exchange for feedback and testimonials.
- **Free content** - each piece must hook attention, retain it, and reward it. Give far more than you ask; when you ask, make it clear and short. Consistency and volume matter more than polish.
- **Cold outreach** - calls, emails, DMs to strangers. It's a numbers game: build a targeted list, personalise the opening, give big value fast, follow up many times, and use volume.
- **Paid ads** - callout (get the right person to notice), value (why they should care), call to action (exactly what to do next). Scale spend only when a customer returns more than they cost.

**Lead getters.** After the Core Four work, get other people to do them for you: customer referrals, employees, agencies, and affiliates.

**Rule of 100.** Pick one channel and do 100 primary actions every day for 100 days: 100 outreaches, or 100 minutes of content creation, or $100 a day on ads.

**More, Better, New.** When something works, first do more of it, then make it better, and only then try something new. Most people jump to new too early.

**The maths.** Lifetime gross profit per customer (LTGP) must beat CAC, ideally by 3x or more. Best case is client-financed acquisition: gross profit collected in the first 30 days covers the cost of getting that customer, so growth is not limited by cash.

## Part 3: Money Models (*$100M Money Models*)

**Core idea:** a money model is a deliberate sequence of offers that gets customers, makes them profitable fast, and keeps them paying. Target: one customer's first-30-day cash pays to acquire and serve at least two more.

Four kinds of offer, in order:

1. **Attraction offers** - turn strangers into customers.
   - Win your money back (pay, hit the goal, get refunded or credited)
   - Giveaways (one winner, everyone else gets a discount offer)
   - Decoy (a basic free/cheap option beside a much better premium one)
   - Buy X get Y free
   - Pay less now or pay more later
2. **Upsell offers** - increase what they spend, offered right after they buy or get a win.
   - Classic ("you can't have X without Y")
   - Menu (tell them what they don't need, prescribe what they do, ask A or B)
   - Anchor (show the expensive option first, then the main one)
   - Rollover (credit what they already paid toward the bigger thing)
3. **Downsell offers** - turn a "no" into a "yes" without discounting the same thing.
   - Payment plans
   - Trial with a penalty (free or cheap if they meet conditions)
   - Feature downsell (lower price for less: fewer features, less access, lower guarantee)
4. **Continuity offers** - recurring revenue.
   - Continuity bonus (join today and get this valuable bonus)
   - Continuity discount (free or cheaper periods for committing longer)
   - Waived fee (setup fee waived if they commit to a term)

Build one stage at a time: get the attraction offer working, then add an upsell, then a downsell, then continuity. Do not launch all four at once.

## Diagnosis cheat sheet

| Symptom | Likely fix |
|---|---|
| "Nobody knows we exist" | Pick one of the Core Four and apply the Rule of 100 |
| Leads come in but don't buy | Rebuild the offer with the value equation; add guarantee and bonuses |
| Customers say it's too expensive | Raise perceived value, niche down, add a downsell; do not just cut price |
| Ads are unprofitable | Raise 30-day cash per customer with upsells before spending more |
| Sales are one-off | Add a continuity offer |
| Growing but always short on cash | Aim for client-financed acquisition |
| Trying five channels, none working | Cut to one. More, then better, then new |
| Competing on price | Change the offer so it can't be compared like-for-like |
| Calls happen but don't close | CLOSER structure and AAA for objections (see `courses-and-videos.md`) |
| Customers leave after a month or two | Fix onboarding and the first 90 days before buying more leads |
| Unsure what to focus on | Find their stage on the Scaling Roadmap and fix that one constraint |

## Rules

- Scarcity, urgency, guarantees, and testimonials must be true. Never suggest fake deadlines, fake stock limits, or made-up results.
- Do not promise income or results. Frame numbers as targets and tests.
- Work only with the user's real numbers or clearly labelled estimates.
- These frameworks suit businesses selling a product or service. If the user's situation is outside that, say so and adapt carefully.
- For legal, tax, and regulated-industry questions (refund law, financial promotions, health claims), flag that they need a qualified professional.
