## Sales (from his videos and sales training)

**CLOSER - the structure of a sales conversation [check: step wording varies by source]**

- **C**larify why they are here. Ask what made them reach out now.
- **L**abel the problem. Say back the gap between where they are and where they want to be, and get them to agree.
- **O**verview their past. Ask what they've tried and why it didn't work.
- **S**ell the vacation. Describe the outcome and experience, not the process (the destination, not the flight). Three main points at most.
- **E**xplain away concerns. Handle the objections (see below).
- **R**einforce the decision. Make them feel good about buying right after they do; onboarding starts here.

**AAA - answering any objection [solid]**

- **Acknowledge** - repeat the concern back calmly so they feel heard. Do not argue.
- **Associate** - connect it to what your best customers did or said in the same spot.
- **Ask** - ask a question that moves the conversation forward. The person asking questions leads.

**The onion of blame - what objections really are [solid]**

Prospects move through layers of reasons not to buy, from outside themselves inward. Handle them in this order:

1. **Circumstances** - time ("I'm too busy"), money ("it's too expensive"), fit ("it won't work for me")
2. **Other people** - authority ("I need to ask my partner")
3. **Self** - avoidance ("I need to think about it")

Underlying rule: selling is helping someone make a decision, yes or no. A clear "no" is fine; "maybe" is the failure.

## Making each customer worth more (videos)

**Six ways to raise lifetime gross profit [solid]**

1. Raise the price
2. Lower the cost of delivering
3. Get them to buy more often
4. Upsell a better version of the same thing
5. Cross-sell a different thing they also need
6. Downsell a cheaper option to those who would otherwise leave or not buy

He treats price as the strongest profit lever of all: a price change usually moves profit more than the same percentage gain in customers or retention.

**Retention and churn [solid on the maths, check on benchmarks]**

- Recurring-revenue lifetime value = monthly gross profit per customer ÷ monthly churn rate. Halving churn doubles lifetime value.
- A business stops growing when customers lost each month equal customers gained. Fix the leak before pouring in more leads.
- Most churn happens early. Focus on getting customers past the first 90 days, then past month six.
- Add a real onboarding process right after purchase so the customer gets a first win fast.
- Ask every leaving customer why they left, and ask your longest-staying customers why they stay. Do more of the second.
- Rough benchmarks mentioned in his content: around 80% monthly retention is average and 90%+ is good for many subscription businesses; for B2B, 80%+ kept per year. [check]

## *$100M Lost Chapters* [check: from audiobook episode summaries]

- **Your first avatar** - how to choose which customer to serve first; the most important selection decision.
- **Section A: Attract** - more attraction offers, including free "pick your price", freemium, and free presentations.
- **Section B: The expensive customer problem** - the maths of customer-financed acquisition (CFA):
  - **CAC** - what it costs in total to get one customer.
  - **LTGP** - lifetime gross profit per customer.
  - **Payback period** - how long until a customer's gross profit repays their CAC. Shorter means faster reinvestment.
  - Three levers of CFA: lower the cost to acquire, raise gross profit per customer, collect that profit sooner.
  - Levels of CFA: from "a customer eventually pays back their cost" up to "a customer pays for more than one new customer within 30 days".
- **Section C: Advanced offer stacking** - the back end and the "value grid" (laying out all offers to see CAC against 30-day cash), plus more continuity ideas such as lifetime discounts and lifetime upgrades.
- **Section D: Employees** - an expanded version of the employees chapter from *$100M Leads*, on getting a team to generate leads.

## The Scaling Roadmap [stages 1-6 solid; 0 and 7-9 check]

Each stage has one main constraint. Fix that, ignore the rest, then move to the next.

| Stage | Name | The job at this stage |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Improvise | Find something people will pay for |
| 1 | Monetize | Sell it yourself, by hand, until sales are repeatable |
| 2 | Advertise | Make lead flow consistent (Rule of 100, one channel) |
| 3 | Stabilize | Get help; write down processes so delivery doesn't depend on you |
| 4 | Prioritize | Commit to one customer type and one offer; track the numbers |
| 5 | Productize | Make delivery repeatable; add upsells; reduce churn |
| 6 | Optimize | Operating discipline: management, metrics, margins |
| 7 | Categorize | Build real departments and leaders |
| 8 | Specialize | Deep specialist roles and systems |
| 9 | Capitalize | Use capital and acquisitions to grow |

Common failure he and Leila Hormozi stress: companies die from doing too many things, not from too few opportunities.
